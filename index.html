04 / Mathematical notes

Definitions, caveats and interpretation needed to read the numerical experiments correctly.

Riemann viewpoint

∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx ≈ Σ f(ξᵢ) Δxᵢ

The partition is made in the domain. Midpoint and trapezoidal rules are used here as practical quadrature schemes. For continuous functions, their errors decrease as the mesh is refined.

Lebesgue viewpoint

∫ f dμ ≈ Σ cⱼ μ({x : f(x) ∈ Bⱼ})

The partition is made in the range; each bin is weighted by the measure of its preimage. This implementation estimates those measures by counting domain samples in each bin.

Normal CDF and erf

Φ(z) = ½ [1 + erf(z/√2)]

erf is a special function, defined by an integral. It gives an exact analytical representation, but its numerical value still requires an algorithm or a high-quality library.

Liouville's theorem

Liouville's theorem in differential algebra gives criteria for an elementary antiderivative. For exp(−x²), it would require a rational function R satisfying R′ − 2xR = 1. No such rational function exists: poles are impossible, so R would be polynomial, but its highest-degree term cannot cancel to produce 1. Thus the Gaussian antiderivative is not elementary.
